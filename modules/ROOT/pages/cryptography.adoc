A systematic method of encrypting and decrypting messages or data.
It is used in xref:computer-security.adoc[Computer Security] by protecting Data Communications and xref:computer-networking.adoc[Computer Networking].

Fully integrates xref:cia-triad.adoc[CIA Triad], that must be achieved at all cost:

* Confidentiality
* Integrity
* Availability

Cryptography is not limited to encrypting messages where xref:plain-text.adoc[Plain Text] is converted into xref:cipher-text.adoc[Cipher Text], but instead a broader concept that involves other processes necessary for securing messages such as xref:encryption.adoc[Encryption], Hashing, and Authentication.

== History

=== Paper Era

A "pen and ink" period.
Codes in this era is relatively simple because messages had to be decoded by hand.
Not very secure compared to modern standards.

Because of common cipher types xref:substitution-cipher.adoc[Substitution Cipher] and xref:transposition-cipher.adoc[Transposition Cipher].

=== Mechanical Era

* Enigma machine

=== Modern Era

Heavy reliance on mathematics and electronic computers.

* One-Time Pad
* Data Encryption Standard by National Institute for Standards in Technology

== Confidentiality, Integrity, and Authenticity

=== Confidentiality

The goal is to prevent everyone except the intended entity from reading or tampering a message, known as xref:plain-text.adoc[Plain Text], by transforming the message into an encrypted message, known as a xref:cipher-text.adoc[Cipher Text].

If the message is *confidential*, then the attacker does not know its contents.

=== Integrity

If the message has *integrity*, then the attacker cannot change its contents without being detected.

=== Authenticity

If the message has *authenticity*, then it's guaranteed that the message was written by the entity who claims to have written it.
