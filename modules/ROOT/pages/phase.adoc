'''

The term phase describes the position of the waveform relative to time 0. If we think of the waves as something that can be shifted backward or forward along the time axis, phase describes the amount of that shift.

Phase is measured in degrees or radians.

* latexmath:[360\degree] latexmath:[2n] rad
* latexmath:[1\degree] is latexmath:[\frac{2\pi}{360\degree}] rad
* latexmath:[1] rad is latexmath:[\frac{360}{2n}\degree]

*Phase Shifts*:

* a phase shift of latexmath:[360\degree] corresponds to a shift of a complete period
* a phase shift of latexmath:[180\degree] corresponds to a shift of one-half of a period
* a phase shift of latexmath:[90\degree] corresponds to a shift of one-quarter of a period

[NOTE]
====
image::phase-waves-3-sine-waves-with-same-amplitude-and-frequency-but-different-phase.png[]
====

* (a) A sine wave with a phase of latexmath:[0\degree] starts at time 0 with a zero amplitude. The amplitude is _increasing_.
* (b.) A sine wave with a phase of latexmath:[90\degree] starts at time 0 with a peak amplitude. The amplitude is _decreasing_.
* (c.) A sine wave with a phase of latexmath:[180\degree] starts at time 0 with a zero amplitude. The amplitude is _decreasing_.

Another way to look at the phase is in terms of _shift_ or _offset_. We can say that:

* (a.) A sine wave with a phase of latexmath:[0\degree] is not shifted.
* (b.) A sine wave with a phase of latexmath:[90\degree] is shifted to the left by 1/4 cycle. However, note that the signal does not really exist before time 0.
* (c.) A sine wave with a phase of latexmath:[180\degree] is shifted to the left by 1/2 cycle. However, note that the signal does not really exist before time 0.

'''
